01 / THE OPPORTUNITY

A better way to order at the table.

The original Cozy Cafe project covered menu browsing, cart review and order tracking. The redesign expands that flow to account for the parts of a café visit it left out: joining a table, ordering another round, requesting help, and paying as a group.

The interface is deliberately quieter than a delivery app. Menu details and prices take priority over promotions; after ordering, guests can put their phones down and return only when needed.

PRODUCT

Cozy Cafe 2.0

PLATFORM

Responsive mobile web

PROJECT TYPE

Independent concept redesign

SCOPE

Guest ordering and table billing

DESIGN OUTPUTS

Wireframes, 28 interface states, prototype

VALIDATION STATUS

Concept design; usability testing pending

02 / PRODUCT STRATEGY

Design for the entire visit.

A dine-in order is not always a single transaction. Someone may arrive late, a table may order in two rounds, and people may want separate bills. Those scenarios shape the navigation and the order model.

01

Start at the table

Use a table QR and lightweight guest names instead of requiring registration before customers can see the menu.

Design decisionSession confirmation precedes menu browsing.
02

Check before sending

Show selected options, exact item prices, and a final review before sending anything to the kitchen.

Design decisionCart confirmation is separate from payment.
03

Track who ordered what

Guests can place separate orders in the same table session, review assigned items, and divide shared costs.

Design decisionThe table bill clearly distinguishes paid and outstanding amounts.
Project scope

This is a self-directed redesign based on a review of the earlier interface and common dine-in ordering scenarios. The decisions shown are design hypotheses, not findings from conducted interviews or measured usability studies.

PRODUCT RULES / DESIGN HANDOFF

Product rules behind the screens.

The screens are built around a proposed set of ordering and billing rules. These would need confirmation with café staff and an implementation team.

01 / SESSION

One table, multiple guests

A table QR creates a visit context. Each guest has their own cart and order ownership; the table can still see an aggregate bill.

02 / INVENTORY

Verify before submission

Availability, modifiers and prices should be checked against current kitchen inventory when an order is submitted.

03 / ORDERS

Submission is not preparation

Order received, accepted, preparing, ready and served are distinct states. An uncertain submission cannot silently become a confirmed order.

04 / PAYMENTS

Never confuse payment states

Authorised, pending, failed and confirmed are distinct. Bill updates invalidate an unsubmitted split and require review.

05 / ACCESSIBILITY

Readable and operable

Target WCAG 2.2 AA, touch targets of at least 24px with suitable spacing, visible focus, semantic labels and reduced-motion support. Validate rather than assume compliance.

06 / RESEARCH

Hypotheses, not fabricated proof

The scenarios, menu, prices and customer journey are illustrative design assumptions. Research, usability findings and performance improvements are not claimed.

05 / PRODUCT LOGIC

How the ordering model works.

One table, separate carts

Guests can share a visit while each keeps control of their own selections. Their orders come together for tracking and billing.

One submission, one order

Order confirmation and recovery states should prevent accidental duplicate kitchen tickets when a connection is interrupted.

No premature success

Pending, failed, and completed payments stay visibly distinct. Session closure is possible only once outstanding charges are settled.

Design and evidence boundaries. This is a concept redesign based on a proposed café operating model. Screens, sample names, menu prices, and order details are illustrative. No user interviews, usability outcomes, revenue increases, or operational performance metrics are claimed. Kitchen integrations, payment reconciliation, tax rules, and staff processes require real-world validation.
06 / QUALITY & VALIDATION

What I would test next.

This is a concept project, not a deployed service. Before building it, I would test the ordering flow with guests, review bill-splitting cases, and verify the kitchen workflow with staff.

01 — TASK TESTING

Can guests finish an order without help?

Test first-time QR entry, drink customization, order confirmation, and reordering on a small mobile device.

02 — GROUP BILLING

Does everyone understand who owes what?

Validate shared-item allocation, partial payment, and what happens when a guest leaves before the bill is settled.

03 — OPERATIONAL FLOW

Can the kitchen support these promises?

Confirm stock availability, order status ownership, error recovery, and the timing of staff notifications.

04 — ACCESSIBILITY

Does the experience work for more guests?

Review text contrast, touch-target sizes, keyboard navigation, status announcements, and reduced-motion settings.

Design takeaway

The strongest improvement is not a larger menu or more features. It is a single, legible journey that carries guests from joining a table through ordering, reordering, and settling up—with fewer uncertain moments in between.
